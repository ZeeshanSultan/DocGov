# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is acting in good faith. Critique work, not people.

## What is expected

- **Separate the code from the author.** "This rule will produce false positives on monorepos"
  is useful. "You clearly don't understand monorepos" is not, and it is also less informative.
- **Bring the evidence.** A disagreement settled by a reproduction, a benchmark or a quote from
  the documentation is settled. One settled by persistence is not settled.
- **Accept that maintainers say no.** Most proposals here will be declined for the same reason:
  a rule that fires on documentation people consider fine costs more than it is worth. That is
  a judgement about the product, not about you.
- **Let people be wrong in public without punishing them for it.** That is how anyone learns a
  codebase.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, demeaning or discriminatory comments, personal or political attacks, publishing
others' private information, sustained disruption, and unwelcome sexual attention. This applies
in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments and any other project space.

Raising the temperature deliberately — baiting, sarcasm aimed at a person, relitigating a
closed decision to wear someone down — is also not acceptable, even when every individual
message is technically polite.

## Scope

Project spaces, and public spaces when someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Email the maintainer, or open a [private security advisory](https://github.com/ZeeshanSultan/DocGov/security/advisories/new)
if you need a confidential channel and have no other one. Reports are handled privately.

Maintainers will respond with what fits: a private word, a warning, deleting a comment, or a
ban. Maintainers who do not follow this themselves should be reported the same way, and the
same consequences apply to them.

Adapted in spirit from the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org), written
plainly rather than quoted.
